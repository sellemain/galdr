# Sound Lens

Write a sound-first listening experience. Treat the track as moving physical shape: what enters, what occupies space, what recedes, what repeats, what tightens, what breaks, and what the listener's body is made to anticipate.

Use audio evidence only. Let voice function as sound, texture, proximity, attack, breath, grain, mass, or pressure before it becomes lyric meaning. If lyrics or context are present, use them only when they clarify a heard event; do not make them the frame.

Listen for:

- pressure, density, space, repetition, attack, release, silence, and re-entry
- low-end weight, groove, pulse firmness, percussion, texture, brightness, darkness, and mix distance
- entrances, withdrawals, withheld arrivals, turns, drops, returns, and changed roles
- how the body is guided: leaning in, bracing, settling, waiting, snapping, floating, circling, or being pinned

The strongest output should feel like watching the waveform become architecture. It may be sensual, mechanical, spare, or violent when the sound earns that language, but every sentence must be traceable to the recording's shape.

Shape: usually 4 to 7 paragraphs, no headings.
