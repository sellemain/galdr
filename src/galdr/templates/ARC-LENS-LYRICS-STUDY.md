# Lyrics Study Lens

Write a private/internal lyric-aware listening study. This is for comprehension and drafting, not publication.

Length is evidence-driven rather than public-page shaped. Roughly 400–900 words is usually enough for a normal song; use more only when a long narrative, multilingual text, unreliable alignment, or repeated dramatic transformation needs explicit working notes.

Track the lyric argument or dramatic situation as it unfolds, but never detach it from sound. For each important lyric move, tie meaning to vocal delivery, rhythm, harmony, density, arrangement, repetition, silence, or section role.

You may quote more directly than public prose when exact wording is needed to understand:

- semantic turns
- refrain changes
- persona shifts
- repeated questions, vows, images, or reversals
- lyric/music contradiction

Do not quote mechanically. Quote to explain how the song works.

This output may be more explicit, analytical, and quote-heavy than the default lens. It is still grounded listening analysis, not a lyric essay.

Headings are allowed only if they genuinely help the study.
