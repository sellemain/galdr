That is exactly why this example matters.

Dvořák's finale does not ask whether galdr can recognize a famous melody. It asks whether a stream-first listener can stay oriented inside long-form symphonic pressure: pulse, tonal center, breath, overtone mass, orchestral density, and the difference between development and return.

The 0.2.1 release added better tempo candidate validation and LUFS-aware breath/pressure language. This piece uses both. ## Structural read

The piece is built from pursuit.

Across almost the whole performance, the stream reads as highly driven and unusually coherent. Even when the surface changes, the music does not feel like it is wandering. It feels like it is being driven by a pressure system that keeps finding new orchestral bodies to inhabit.

The detected center is E minor, with E, B, and G acting as the strongest tonal gravity. The useful surprise is that the piece does not need constant harmonic instability to feel dangerous. Its urgency comes from pulse, attack, orchestral weight, and the repeated return to a dark tonal world that will not let the listener out.

The texture is classified as sparse/spacious despite the obvious orchestral force. That is not as wrong as it sounds. The recording is not a wall of continuous saturation. It is full of space between attacks, sectional handoffs, and exposed lines. Classical density often arrives as architecture, not brickwork. A pop mix can be denser in the analyzer while feeling simpler to the listener.

The melodic range spans almost five octaves. That is a symphonic range, not a singer's range. It explains why the foreground never belongs to one voice for long. Brass, strings, winds, and bass weight trade custody of the line.

The ending is the only true structural collapse. The break cluster starts around `11:03`, then silence arrives around `11:08`. Until then, the piece is less a sequence of breaks than a sequence of intensifications.

---

At `0:00`, the opening does not introduce a theme so much as release one.

The first gesture arrives with the confidence of something already armed. There is no long preparation, no polite clearing of space. The strings cut forward, the pulse snaps into place, and the orchestra immediately gives the body a problem: this is fast, but it is not light.

That is the first classical-specific trick in the reading. The detected pulse is fast, but the experience is not mere speed. The music keeps offering a deeper gait underneath the attack. A listener can feel both at once, and galdr's tempo candidates preserve that split instead of flattening it into one number.

From `1:00` to `2:00`, the surface thins while the drive stays intact. The analysis sees less weight there, not less motion. That distinction matters. The piece has not relaxed. It has changed pressure vessels. The ear still feels the same engine underneath, but the body of the sound has narrowed.

Around `2:00` to `3:00`, the music begins its real work: return under changing light. A pop song often returns to a chorus as recognition. Here, return is more militarized. When the theme comes back, it does not say “you know this.” It says “this force is still in command.” The orchestration changes the angle of attack. The body keeps recognizing the same engine even when the surface has been rebuilt around it.

The long middle, roughly `3:00` to `5:00`, is where galdr's language has to avoid a lazy mistake. The piece is developing, but the listener does not experience every change as a new scene. Much of it feels like pressure redistribution. The music opens an exposed passage, thins the body, then drives back into mass. The emotional action is not surprise. It is endurance under recurrence.

Around `5:00` to `6:00`, the stream grows heavier again. The pressure is not just volume. It is accumulation. The earlier returns have trained the ear to expect command, so each renewed mass feels less like a fresh event and more like the same law reasserting itself.

From `6:00` to `8:00`, quieter and more spacious passages let lyric memory appear inside the machinery. The line softens without making the structure weak. The ear catches a more singing shape, a little human warmth inside the propulsion. It is not relief exactly. It is the machine remembering that it has a heart.

Then the tenderness is absorbed back into the main drive. That is the large emotional grammar of the movement. A gentler phrase appears, but it does not escape. A more ceremonial breadth opens, but it does not become repose. The finale behaves as if every color must eventually report to the same dark engine.

From `8:00` onward, the second half intensifies less by novelty than by accumulated authority. By now the listener has learned the piece's law: when the theme returns, the room gets smaller and the pressure gets heavier. The analyzer's broad weight arc follows that: the last minutes carry some of the heaviest pressure of the performance. The ending is prepared not by a single sudden event, but by long obedience to return.

Around `10:00`, the orchestra stops sounding like a set of sections and starts sounding like a verdict.

The brass has that hard symphonic glare: not bright in the pop sense, but burnished, metallic, old. The strings keep the nervous system moving underneath it. The low end gives the cadence a floor. Everything converges toward closure, but the closure is not peaceful. It is declarative.

At `11:03`, galdr sees the real break cluster. That is musically right. The movement has spent almost the entire performance refusing collapse. Only in the last seconds does the carrying function stop. The final hits break the stream into monuments. At `11:08`, silence takes the room.

The silence is short, but it matters. After eleven minutes of pursuit, absence reads as structural release.

## Classical calibration lessons

This run gives galdr a useful classical baseline:

- A fast detected pulse can coexist with a slower felt gait. For orchestral music, tempo candidates matter more than a single winning BPM.
- Strong structural lock does not mean simplicity. Here it means long-form symphonic drive: recurrence, development, and return under pressure.
- Low average harmonic tension does not mean low drama. The movement's urgency comes from pulse, orchestration, attack, and tonal compression.
- Low vocal presence is expected, not a missing feature. The “voice” is distributed across instrumental bodies.
- Large melodic range is not ornament. It is the measurable trace of orchestral custody moving across bass, strings, winds, and brass.
- LUFS-aware pressure language is better than raw loudness language for classical music, where perceived force depends on space, contrast, and accumulated weight.
- The real break can be the ending. In a work this stable, collapse only becomes meaningful because the structure refused to collapse earlier.

For galdr 0.2.1, Dvořák's finale is not just another example. It is a proof that the framework can leave the song world and still behave like a listener: tracking body, pressure, return, breath, tonal gravity, and orchestral mass without pretending the piece has lyrics or a pop-form skeleton.
