# GAEREA - Submerged

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NpdWlHP_pns

The song opens already underwater, but not quietly. The first pressure arrives as a full surface, bright at the edges and heavy underneath, and the body has to accept the pulse before it can decide whether there is room to breathe. The rain in the video gives the sound an immediate skin: hooded figures, instruments half-swallowed by water and darkness, motion seen through impact rather than clarity.

Then the words name the condition the music has already made: *"We're submerged, and can't break free."* The line does not land as metaphor first. It lands as environment. The rhythm is too steady to feel like panic, too locked to let the listener float away from it. The track is not drowning by losing shape. It is drowning by keeping shape under pressure.

The chorus image keeps tightening: *"The tide of our story's got a hold on me."* The music gives that hold a mechanical regularity. Every return feels less like a new wave than the same force arriving again with the patience of something larger than the person inside it. In the video, a body reaches through water and darkness, arm extended toward a surface that stays just out of reach. The frame is obvious, almost literal, but it works because the sound has already done the same thing: reach, contact, no release.

When the lyric turns inward, *"The lies I hide,"* the arrangement does not pull back to confess. It stays large. That matters. The private thing is not exposed by intimacy; it is exposed by being forced to continue inside a massive public weather. *"Drowning in the shadows of my past"* arrives over a pulse that refuses to loosen, and the past becomes less like memory than current. It is not behind the singer. It is the medium he is moving through.

The middle of the song keeps the body pinned while the images narrow. A hand presses against rain-covered glass. A figure stands with arms open under falling water. The video keeps returning to surfaces that can be touched but not crossed: glass, water, glare, wet shadow. The track does the same structurally. It gives the listener hard edges and repeated entries, but almost no escape route. Even the melodic motion leans downward more often than it rises, as if every lift has to pay back gravity.

*"These ghosts are pulling me beneath"* is the cleanest statement of the song's motion. The word ghosts could have made the track theatrical, but the music keeps it physical. The pull is not spooky. It is downward force. The next line, *"Gasping for air, but I can't breathe,"* comes into a mix that is still moving with brutal confidence, and the contradiction becomes the point: the body is gasping inside a machine that never gasps.

By the time *"I'm submerged, oh, can't you see?"* returns, the question is almost accusatory. The listener has been inside the condition long enough that seeing is no longer the problem. The problem is that recognition does not break the water. *"The waves of my past are the chains on me"* gives the song its strongest fusion of image and weight: water behaving like metal, memory behaving like restraint. The video answers with silhouettes behind wet glass, hands raised close to the surface, present but blurred into the barrier.

At *"I see the memories / Circling below,"* the music does not open into reflection. It rotates. The repeated *"Circling below"* turns the lyric into a trapdoor that does not finish falling. Attention keeps returning to the same depth, the same pressure, the same held pattern. The track's steadiness becomes merciless here. It is not chaos. It is recurrence.

Around 3:28, the video gives the most literal version of the whole piece: a figure underwater reaching upward, bubbles trailing through the dark. The song has already earned the image by refusing to let the pulse become rescue. The reach is real, but the surface remains elsewhere. The body moves; the water owns the frame.

Near 4:39, the structure starts to fracture at the edge. The breaks are not explosions. They feel like the system stuttering after holding too long, particles suspended in black, the image reduced almost to debris. Then the final return gathers the whole premise again: *"We're submerged, and can't break free,"* *"We're lost in the deep where the waters flow."* The last sung sounds stretch into open vowels, not release exactly, more like the voice losing language before it loses force.

At 5:05, the video cuts from obscured figures to black as the audio drops into silence. This is not an ornate ending. It is the only ending the song has prepared: water, barrier, pressure, then no visible surface at all.
